Where things stand

  • TB 1 is empty. TB 2 holds the Mokin USB4 enclosure with a 2 TB NVMe drive, stable at 40 Gb/s. TB 4 has the USB dock.
  • The mini runs one bus-powered NVMe enclosure reliably, not two. See the troubleshooting section below.
  • The MacBook Pro is no longer cabled to the mini. Its own page has the details.

The 4 GB write on the P310 stays inside its fast cache. In a longer run, writes fall to about 0.9 GB/s once the cache fills, which is the limit of its QLC flash. Temperature peaked at 70 °C after about 400 GB of nonstop writes and never throttled. The dock SSD was measured shortly after a reboot, slightly under its usual ~850 MB/s. Temperature and wear come from smartctl, which cannot read the USB-bridged drives.

Thunderbolt troubleshooting

Two NVMe enclosures, two ports, one afternoon.

SetupResult
Mokin on TB 1, ACASIS secondACASIS fell back to USB 10 Gb/s and its 990 PRO ran at about 950 MB/s. A new cable and a reboot did not help.
Swapped: ACASIS on TB 1, Mokin secondACASIS came up at 40 Gb/s (2792 MB/s write, 3021 read). The Mokin's drive failed to attach, mounted unwritable, then dropped. No PCIe or NVMe errors were logged first.
Mokin alone on TB 2Stable at 40 Gb/s, 3043 MB/s write and 3667 read.
  • The second enclosure to connect loses. Each Thunderbolt port supplies bus power of about 15 W. The likely cause is that two bus-powered enclosures don't fit, though the logs can't prove it: overcurrent counters stayed at 0.
  • TB 2 is not faulty. The Mokin runs the same speed on TB 1 and TB 2.
  • If both are ever needed: put one behind a self-powered Thunderbolt hub.
  • Eject before unplugging. The P310 has 31 unsafe shutdowns on record, several from this troubleshooting.

Thunderbolt 4 (2020)40 Gb/sThe mini's three back ports

Both enclosures run a 40 Gb/s link, but inside the Mokin the USB4 chip reaches the SSD at PCIe Gen4 (about 3.7 GB/s here) while the ACASIS Thunderbolt 3 chip runs Gen3 (about 3 GB/s), even with a faster drive. Neither gets faster on a Thunderbolt 5 port.

Ethernet carries the traffic. Wi-Fi has a strong signal but latency that swings from 2.5 to 113 ms. The October 19 scan found Wi-Fi on 5 GHz (channel 157, 80 MHz) at 720 Mb/s, not the 6 GHz band of the earlier test.

The M6 can drive three displays, including two 4K screens at 165 Hz. A pair of 4K 144 to 165 Hz monitors is the next upgrade.

The dock

  • An Ivanky Fusion Dock 3. It is a USB dock, not Thunderbolt, so everything behind it shares one 10 Gb/s link and macOS lists its Thunderbolt port as empty.
  • HDMI up to 4K at 144 Hz, a 10 Gb and a 5 Gb USB hub, an SD card reader, and an M.2 slot that holds a 512 GB SSD used for scratch files. It is powered from a separate 100 W USB-C supply.
  • Also behind it: the 4 TB Time Machine drive and a Blu-ray/DVD burner on USB 2.0.
