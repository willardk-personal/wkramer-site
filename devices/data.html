fio with direct I/O. Random 4k at queue depth 32 (70% read): 113k read and 48k write operations per second, the same as in the ACASIS. That is more than twice the internal SSD (47k and 20k) in the same test. A retest that evening, after the move to the 15 W port, gave 3,490 MB/s write and 3,624 read. Media, which did not move, dropped by the same 5%, so that is run-to-run spread, not the port.

Heat

The Mokin has no fan. Its thermal pad presses the drive against the aluminum case, which works as the heatsink, so the case feels warm in use. The 3-minute sustained write rewrote a 64 GB file, 661 GB in all, at a steady 3,674 MB/s. The drive went from 31 to 42 °C, far below where it would slow down.

Thunderbolt 3 against USB4

StandardSpeedIn this setup
Thunderbolt 3 (2015)40 Gb/sThe ACASIS enclosure DATA used until November 13

Thunderbolt 4 (2020)40 Gb/sThe mini's three back ports
Thunderbolt 5 (2024)80 or 120 Gb/sThe Wavlink dock, held to 40 Gb/s by the mini

Both enclosures hold a 40 Gb/s link. The difference is inside. The ACASIS Thunderbolt 3 chip reached the 990 PRO at PCIe Gen3, about 3.2 GB/s. The Mokin's USB4 chip runs it at Gen4, so reads rose from 3,170 to 3,831 MB/s and writes from 2,930 to 3,660 MB/s. About 3.8 GB/s is the most any enclosure gets on the mini's 40 Gb/s ports. Neither enclosure would get faster on a Thunderbolt 5 port.
