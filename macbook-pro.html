Mac Work Book Pro*

An Apple M5 Pro MacBook Pro with 48 GB of memory at the home desk. One USB-C cable to the Ivanky dock carries a monitor, both backup drives and the camera; the second monitor is on the MacBook's own HDMI port, and a Mokin 140 W charger powers it all.

Worth remembering

One 10 Gb/s link, and that's enough

The Ivanky is a USB-C dock, not Thunderbolt. Both backup drives, the card reader and the camera share its single 10 Gb/s link. The SanDisk still reads at 780 MB/s, close to what it managed on its own port.

Video rides its own wires

The dock's monitor runs over DisplayPort Alt Mode, separate from the USB data, so it doesn't slow the drives. The second monitor sits on the MacBook's own HDMI port.

Two computers, no KVM

Each monitor has both Macs plugged in at once: the Mac mini on DisplayPort, this MacBook on HDMI. The monitor's input button picks the computer.

One charger for everything

A Mokin 140 W smart charger runs the MacBook over MagSafe and the dock, and shows the power draw. With several devices on it, the 140 W is split, so the MacBook gets less than it would alone.

A $50 dock replaced a $350 one

The Anker Thunderbolt 5 dock is going back. The Ivanky, the Mac mini's old dock, gives the same everyday setup. What it gives up: Thunderbolt speed for fast drives, and wired Ethernet.
