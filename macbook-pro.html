Mac Work Book Pro*

An Apple M5 Pro MacBook Pro with 48 GB of memory on one Thunderbolt 5 cable: the dock powers it and carries its drives, wired network, camera and two monitors. Read October 19, 2026, updated that evening with the dock, drive tests and monitor switches.

* I wish I owned this. Alas, it's on loan from work.

Free on the dock: one USB-A port, the SD and microSD slots and the audio jack. Each monitor sits on its own HDMI switch: one side goes to this dock, the other to the Mac mini. There is no KVM, and which monitor is on TB 2 or TB 3 isn't recorded.

InsideNot publishedIntel and Parade USB 3 hubs, a Fresco Logic USB 2 hub and an Infineon dock controller. They're how four USB-A ports and the network adapter share the dock's links.

One Thunderbolt 5 cable carries power and everything else. Everything behind the dock shares its 80 Gb/s upstream, and the USB-A devices also share the Intel hub's 10 Gb/s. That is why the SanDisk and Seagate slow each other during a backup. macOS does not say which physical USB-A socket a device uses.

The cable made the difference: on the dock's USB-A ports the non-SanDisk cable raised write speed from about 450-580 to about 710-730 MB/s. Plugged straight into the Mac it is only slightly faster.

Backups and network

  • Time Machine has two destinations on the dock: the SanDisk SSD, chosen for portability and toughness, and the Seagate hard drive. The latest Seagate backup ran 19:42 to 20:49 and the drive holds 560 GB.
  • The wired link measured 1.74 Gb/s down and 2.11 Gb/s up with networkQuality, with 12 ms idle latency. A test to the Mac mini over the LAN gave 2.35 Gb/s one way (56 retransmits) and 1.86 Gb/s the other (none). Earlier that day it measured 1.60 down and 2.00 up.
  • The two Macs are on the LAN only. The direct Thunderbolt link between them that worked on 17 October is not connected now.

A drive that moves around

  • The ACASIS enclosure with the 990 PRO lived on the Mac mini for a while. It has a Thunderbolt 3 chip plus a USB fallback, so it dropped to USB speed when the mini couldn't power two enclosures at once.
  • On this self-powered dock it holds 40 Gb/s. The full story is in the Mac mini troubleshooting notes.
  • A Thunderbolt 5 port doesn't make it faster. The enclosure's chip and its link both top out at 40 Gb/s.

On the Mac itself

  • A Magic Keyboard with Touch ID, on the Mac's own USB bus rather than through the dock. Keys go over Bluetooth or USB.
  • The iPhone's microphone is available through Continuity, next to the MacBook's own mic and speakers.
  • Memory at the time of reading: about 20.9 GiB active, 20.9 GiB inactive, 3.6 GiB wired, no swap.
