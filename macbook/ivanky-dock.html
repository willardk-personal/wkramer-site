Ivanky 13‑in‑1 USB‑C dock

One USB-C cable to the MacBook. The Ivanky 13-in-1 dock that used to sit under the Mac mini now carries one monitor, both Time Machine drives, the wired network adapter, a card reader and the camera. It replaced an Anker Thunderbolt 5 dock in late November.

What it is

ModelIvanky 13-in-1 Mac mini dock and stand (Fusion Dock 3)
TypeA 10 Gb/s USB-C dock, not Thunderbolt. macOS's Thunderbolt report lists no device, which is expected.
Link to the MacBookThe MacBook's Thunderbolt port on the HDMI edge, used as a plain USB-C port. macOS sees a VIA hub (2109:0822) at 10 Gb/s on a USB 3.1 bus.
VideoHDMI out, up to 4K at 144 Hz. The picture travels over DisplayPort Alt Mode on its own wires in the cable, so it takes nothing from the 10 Gb/s the USB devices share.
PowerIt came with a 20 W adapter, which isn't used. It runs from the same Mokin 140 W smart charger as the MacBook, which shows the power draw.
USB 3 insideVIA 2109:0822 at 10 Gb/s, feeding a VIA 2109:0817 at 5 Gb/s
USB 2 insideVIA 2822 → VIA 2817, plus two Genesys 05e3:0608 hubs, at 480 Mb/s
HistoryThe same unit sat under the Mac mini until early November, with the Scratch Disk in its M.2 slot.

One 10 Gb/s link for everything

The SanDisk, the 2.5 GbE adapter, the Seagate, the card reader, the camera and the M.2 slot all share one 10 Gb/s link to the MacBook. The SanDisk alone can use most of it, so a backup to it and a backup to the Seagate at the same time would share it. A full-speed network transfer takes about 2.5 Gb/s of it. Video doesn't count against it.

The docks before this one

From November 6 to 26 this desk used an Anker Prime 14-in-1 Thunderbolt 5 dock (A83B5). It linked at 80 Gb/s, or 120 Gb/s with both monitors on, carried both monitors over USB-C to HDMI cables and the wired network, and had its power supply built in. It is being returned. Before that, a Wavlink WL-UTD58 Thunderbolt 5 dock was here; it now serves the Mac mini.
