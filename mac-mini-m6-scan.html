Handoff for the Mac mini as it stands tonight: what is attached, how fast each drive is, and what today's Thunderbolt troubleshooting found. Devices that are not plugged in right now are left off the maps. All checks were read-only except short speed-test files, which were deleted.

Where things stand

Current state

  • TB2: Mokin USB4 enclosure with the Crucial P310 2 TB, 40 Gb/s, stable, ~3.0 GB/s write and ~3.7 GB/s read.
  • TB1: empty.
  • TB4: Ivanky dock as plain USB 10 Gb/s, with Scratch Disk, the Time Machine drive, card reader, Blu-ray burner and an HDMI adapter.
  • Display: HP 23er on the mini's HDMI. Nothing is on the dock's HDMI right now.
  • Network: 2.5GbE wired first, Wi-Fi 7 on 5 GHz, Tailscale up.

Findings and open items

  • The mini runs one bus-powered NVMe enclosure reliably, not two. Whichever enclosure connected second fell back to USB or dropped its drive.
  • The ACASIS TBU405Pro moved back to the MacBook's 30 W Wavlink TB5 dock, where it is stable at 40 Gb/s.
  • TB2 is not faulty: no PCIe or NVMe errors, overcurrent counters at 0, and the Mokin is stable there.
  • Open: the P310 now has 31 unsafe shutdowns on record. Eject before unplugging.
  • Open: if both enclosures are ever needed on the mini, put one behind a self-powered Thunderbolt hub.

Pattern: the second enclosure to connect loses. Each mini Thunderbolt port is bus-powered at 15 W, and the two enclosures never both worked at once. The ACASIS has a Thunderbolt 3 chip plus a Realtek USB fallback, so when it comes up short on power it drops to USB instead of failing. Overcurrent counters stayed at 0, so this is a likely explanation that the logs can't prove. TB2 itself checked out: the Mokin is stable there. Next step if both are needed: a self-powered Thunderbolt hub.

Figure 7 · USB4 versus Thunderbolt 3, as it applies here

StandardSpeedWhat it isHere
Thunderbolt 3 · 201540 Gb/sIntel's standard, Intel controller chips. Carries PCIe and DisplayPort.ACASIS TBU405Pro (now on the MacBook)
USB4 v1 · 201920/40 Gb/sTB3's protocol as an open USB standard. Any chipmaker can build it.Mokin (ASMedia 246x)
Thunderbolt 4 · 202040 Gb/sUSB4 with full speed, PCIe and TB3 support required.The mini's three back ports
Thunderbolt 5 · 202480/120 Gb/sUSB4 v2 with the top speeds required.The MacBook and the Wavlink dock

Both enclosures run on a 40 Gb/s link. The difference is inside: the Mokin's USB4 chip talks to the SSD at PCIe Gen4 (~3.7 GB/s here), while the ACASIS's Thunderbolt 3 chip runs Gen3 (~3 GB/s), even with the faster 990 PRO in it. Neither gets faster on a TB5 port.

Appendix · Commands to repeat these checks

# links: Thunderbolt, NVMe, USB
system_profiler SPThunderboltDataType SPNVMeDataType SPUSBHostDataType

# drive temp, wear, unsafe shutdowns (NVMe only; find N with diskutil list external)
smartctl -a -d nvme /dev/diskN

# sequential speed, 4 GB, no cache; delete the file after
fio --name=w --filename=/Volumes/VOL/.fio.bin --rw=write --bs=1m --size=4g --direct=1 --ioengine=posixaio --iodepth=16
fio --name=r --filename=/Volumes/VOL/.fio.bin --rw=read  --bs=1m --size=4g --direct=1 --ioengine=posixaio --iodepth=16

# port connect/drop events; use the full path, zsh has its own `log` builtin
/usr/bin/log show --info --last 30m --style compact --predicate 'process == "kernel" AND eventMessage CONTAINS "TunneledTransport"'

# overcurrent counters (all 0 today)
ioreg -l | grep -i overcurrent
